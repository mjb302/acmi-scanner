Privacy notice

Last updated 6 October 2026. Applies to the ACMI Scanner apps for Android and iOS, version 1.1.

Summary

ACMI Scanner does not collect, store or transmit personal data or patient data. There is no account, no sign-in and no server.

Photographs and recognised text

Text recognition runs on the device (Google ML Kit on Android, Apple Vision on iOS). The photograph is processed in memory and discarded. The recognised text is shown on screen so that you can correct it and is discarded when you scan again or close the app. Nothing is written to the photo library unless you chose a photo from it in the first place, and nothing is uploaded.

Reports

The optional report is a plain text file that you create on demand. It contains the medicines recognised, the scores and the deprescribing prompts, with no patient identifiers. It is handed to the system share sheet; where it goes after that (Files, email, a clinical record) is under your control and your organisation's policies.

Usage counters

The app keeps a small set of anonymous counters on the device only: number of lists scored, number of lists with an ACMI above zero, number of times STOPP criteria were shown, number of times clinical context or the end-of-life step was used, number of results copied, number of medicines marked as deprescribed, number of reports created, and the four outcome answers. These are integers with no text. They are shown on the About screen and are sent only if you choose to email them or include them in a feedback form.

Feedback

The feedback form opens your own email app with the form text filled in, addressed to the author. Nothing is sent by the app itself. Please do not include patient details in feedback.

Permissions

Camera: to photograph a medication list. Photo library (iOS) or the system photo picker (Android): to choose an existing image. No other permissions are requested.

Third parties

No analytics, advertising or crash-reporting services are included. The Android app bundles Google ML Kit text recognition, which runs offline; the iOS app uses Apple's Vision framework on the device.
